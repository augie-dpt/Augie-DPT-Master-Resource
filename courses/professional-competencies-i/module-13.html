TOPIC 13.1

Finding Your Mission

1. Why Bother? Burnout.

  • Dr. Andreyo's answer to "who cares — I paid tuition to learn anatomy and pass the boards": burnout is rampant in this profession. One Italian cross-sectional study found 45.8% of surveyed PTs affected by or at risk of burnout; other studies report high emotional exhaustion, decreased sense of personal achievement, and depersonalization — and PT students show the same patterns. His own story: burnt out within his first couple of years, ready to leave the profession.
  • No three-step recipe fixes burnout — it's multifactorial. But knowing your mission is the starting point: what makes you get up in the morning, do hard things, and persist when a task demands sacrifice or turns mundane.

2. Start With WHY (Sinek's Golden Circle)

  • Most people and organizations start with WHAT (I want to be a PT), move to HOW (go to PT school, work outpatient), and never reach WHY. Sinek's argument: start from why and work outward. Dr. Andreyo's own why: to empower people, motivate individuals, serve others — from which his how and what follow.

3. Build the Raw Material — Mind Maps

  • A mind map starts with a central concept (your mission) and branches into related ideas, then smaller ones — a place to get thoughts on paper and see relationships. Nobody else ever has to see it. Branch it into the worksheet's three lists:
ListThe questions + his worked examples
ValuesWhat do you value most? (People and relationships · mentorship · integrity)
PassionsWhat are you enthusiastic about — what puts you in flow, losing track of time? (Teaching and the aha moment · hands-on work · maybe a population: peds, older adults, sports)
Skills & abilitiesWhat are you good at — what have people told you is a gift? (Organization · teaching · public speaking · one-on-one work). Notice the overlaps across the three lists — that's your signal

4. Vision, Mission, Just Cause

  • Vision = the long-term picture — what it looks like if you literally envision your accomplished goals. Mission = the attackable now — your current plan of attack. Definitions blur, and the readings draw them differently; Sinek (The Infinite Game) skips both and calls it a just cause. For this week: call it whatever you like, write something that tangibly gives your career and personal future direction.
A just cause must be…Meaning
For somethingAffirmative — never against a person or thing
InclusiveNot narrowed to one niche (not "only inpatient") — make it encompassing, like wellness over one setting
Service orientedThe primary beneficiary is someone other than you
ResilientEndures change — an insurance-policy shift shouldn't squash your mission
IdealisticLofty, maybe frankly unachievable — infinite rather than done-next-year

✍️ Anatomy of a mission statement (his own example)

  • "To EMPOWER OTHERS to pursue a life of WELLNESS AND PURPOSE through SERVANT LEADERSHIP."
  • Starts with a VERB → names WHO benefits → WHAT you help them toward → HOW (your skills, abilities, values).
  • Longer or shorter both work. It must be truly yours — not copy-pasted; the module links example statements for calibration only.
★
The worksheet order is the algorithm: list your values, passions, skills → draft your why statement → compress it into one mission sentence with a verb, a beneficiary, an outcome, and your how. Bring the completed worksheets to sync — the dialogue continues there.
