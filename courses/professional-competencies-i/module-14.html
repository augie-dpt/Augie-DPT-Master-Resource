TOPIC 14.1

Lifelong Learning

  • You entered PT school to learn — the learning doesn't stop at graduation. The profession offers many avenues for advancement, specialization, and ongoing learning; this module's job is to make you conversant with the governing bodies and pathways, then have you map your own interests onto them.

1. The Pathways After Graduation

PathwayWhat it is
ResidencyPost-professional, planned program in a specialty area — clinical practice + mentorship + didactics. Directory at ABPTRFE (American Board of Physical Therapy Residency & Fellowship Education); explore the program directory as required work this week
FellowshipA step beyond residency: subspecialty training for PTs who already have residency training or specialist certification in the area
Board-certified specializationGoverned by ABPTS (American Board of Physical Therapy Specialties) — the required task: view at least TWO specializations you're interested in and learn their requirements (exam + clinical-hours pathways)
Continuing education & the restCon-ed courses, certifications, advanced degrees, teaching, research, mentoring — the Guide's Career Advancement chapter (Ch 5) maps the terrain
🎙
Recall Module 1's numbers for context: the first specialist certification exam ran in 1985, and the profession now counts 10 specialty areas and 380+ residencies / 45+ fellowships. Your anatomy and physio professors here are board-certified specialists — ask them about their paths.

2. Finding YOUR Direction

  • The APTA careers video (9:58) surveys settings and roles; the Discovering Your Professional Interests podcast (23:49) is the reflective companion — identifying future growth areas from what already energizes you. Pair both with your Module 13 mission statement: the mission names the why; the career plan names the what and when.

3. The SMART Goals Worksheet (the plan's engine)

LetterThe worksheet's questions
S — SpecificWhat am I going to do? Why is this important to me?
M — MeasurableHow will I measure success? How will I know I've achieved it?
A — AttainableWhat will I do to achieve this? How will I accomplish it?
R — RelevantIs it worthwhile? Does it fit my values? How does achieving it help me?
T — Time-BoundWhen will I accomplish it? How long am I giving myself?
  • The worksheet's second page is an Action Plan: the goal in one sentence, a finish date, the success measure, steps with time estimates and completion dates, obstacles + planned responses, and helpful tools/resources. This is the same goal discipline from Module 10 (small, meetable, celebrated) — now pointed at your career.
★
A workable first plan: one SMART goal for this trimester (academic), one for the year (professional behavior or skill), and one long-horizon direction (a specialization to explore, with its two ABPTS requirement sets actually read). Write them into the ePortfolio plan with your mission statement on top — that's the integration objectives 3.1–3.4 describe.
