TOPIC 12.1

Cultural Competency

  • Inclusion (APTA Core Value): the PT and PTA create a welcoming and equitable environment for all — a safe space, elevating diverse and minority voices, acknowledging personal biases that may impact patient care, and taking a position of anti-discrimination.

🗣️ The module's anchor quote — Sharon Dunn, past APTA President

  • "Judgment is delivered with folded arms, not open arms. It thrives on bias and stereotypes. It fosters tribalism. It ends conversations rather than starting them… It draws us to the margins of the issue rather than the center, making us critics instead of change agents, more interested in tearing-down than building-up."
  • "We cannot transform society through judgment. Our vision charges us not to stand at a distance and point our fingers at our nation's ills but instead to accept a personal responsibility to try to make a difference."

1. The Working Vocabulary

TermWorking definition (from the videos + Miller & Brogan Ch 8)
Cultural competenceThe ability to interact, communicate, and work effectively with people from cultures and backgrounds different from your own — characteristics, examples, and benefits are the subject of the Helpful Professor video
Cultural Quotient (CQ)Emotional intelligence + cultural nuance: adapting your communication, clinical reasoning, and professional behaviors to the person in front of you (the course's contemporary-concepts thread from Module 5)
The Ch 8 progressionCultural awareness → cultural desire → cultural skill → cultural intelligence — the case studies in the chapter walk each step
DEIDiversity (who is represented) · equity (fair access and opportunity) · inclusion (whether people actually belong once they're in the room)
Implicit biasUnconscious associations that shape decisions without permission — the Highbar Research Round Up segment (minutes 3:40–9:40) covers how it's measured and what it does in clinical practice
CLAS standardsThe HHS National Standards for Culturally and Linguistically Appropriate Services — reviewed in Ch 8 and already met in Module 7 (qualified interpreters, integrating beliefs)
  • Objective 2.1 is explicitly about assessing your OWN biases, stereotypes, and level of cultural competence — the module's work is inward-facing first. The APTA's Developing Diversity video and the Achieving Cultural Competence article connect it to the profession's vision: the PT workforce does not yet mirror the society it serves, and cultural competence is written into the APTA vision statement.

2. The Diversity Statement Brainstorm (worksheet)

  • The premise: stories connect us — each of us has one. Using the prompts, you turn a meaningful experience into a story: a statement of values, a lesson learned, your diversity experience, a challenge conquered, your commitment, future impacts.
PromptWhat it's asking
Your definitionWhat are diversity, equity, and inclusion to you — and how do you tie those philosophies together in everyday life?
Salient identityWhich dimensions of your identity shape what you think, know, and want to learn? Consider your power and privilege
Stepped up / didn'tA time you stepped up — and a time you didn't. How did each feel, and how have they shaped your decisions since?
IntersectionalityWhich overlapping identities make people vulnerable in today's society? Have those groups changed over time?
Your roleHow will your background and experiences inform your future role as a PT — and enrich the community?
🎙
The worksheet's second page is a directory of Augustana support offices (Augie Access, Accessibility, Counseling, DEI office, GSA, Title IX, Violence Prevention) with contacts — it lives in your own Canvas module and the Drive copy of the handout.
